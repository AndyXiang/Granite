In [[Bosonic String|bosonic string theory]], we discuss the bosonic excitation on strings, which is described by a scalar field $X^{\mu}(\sigma,\tau)$ on the world-sheet. Here, in order to introduce fermionic d.o.f., we will use the [[Supersymmetry|supersymmetry]] that relates $X^{\mu}(\sigma,\tau)$ to its fermion partners $\psi^{\mu}(\sigma,\tau)$. In the same manner, we will demonstrate the action  with $N=1$ supersymmetry and the quantization, which gives a consistent string theory in critical dimension $D=10$.

From now, we will use greek letter $\mu,\nu$ for target space indices, lower-case English letter $a,b$ for world-sheet coordinates' indices, and upper-case letter $A,B$ for spinor indices.

## Superspace and superfield
Let's start with the **superspace** $\hat{\Sigma}$, the 2-dimensional base space $\Sigma$ extended by two [[Exterior Algebra|Grassmann coordinates]] $\theta^{A}$ that form a Majorana spinor. A general function $f(\sigma,\theta)$ on the superspace can be expanded as
$$f(\sigma,\theta)=f_{0}(\sigma)+f_{1}^{A}(\sigma)\theta_{A}+\frac{1}{2}f_{2}(\sigma)\theta^{A}\theta_{A}.$$
The gamma matrices on the superspace are $\rho^{a}_{AB},a=0,1$ as
$$\rho^{0}_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -\mathrm{i} \\ \mathrm{i} & 0
\end{pmatrix},\rho^{1}_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}
0 & \mathrm{i}\\\mathrm{i} &   0
\end{pmatrix}.$$

Now we can introduce the **superfield**
$$Y^{\mu}(\sigma,\theta)=X^{\mu}(\sigma)+\bar{\theta} \psi^{\mu}(\sigma)+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\theta}\theta B^{\mu}(\sigma),$$
where $\bar{\theta}=\theta^{\mathsf{T}}\rho^{0}$. So here $X^{\mu},B^{\mu}$ are bosonic fields in target space, and $\psi^{\mu}$ are Majorana spinor fields.

Next, let's show how the supersymmetry is manifested in superspace. Consider the transformation generated by
$$Q_{A}=\frac{\partial }{\partial \bar{\theta}^{A}}+\mathrm{i}\rho^{a}_{AB}\theta^{B}\partial _{a}.$$
Such transformation acts on superspace as
$$\delta_{A}\sigma^{a}=\mathrm{i}\rho^{a}_{AB}\theta^{B},\delta_{A}\theta^{B}=\delta_{A}^{B},\delta_{A}\bar{\theta}_{B}=\rho^{0}_{AB}.$$
The transformation of superfield is as
$$\delta_{A}Y^{\mu}(\sigma,\theta)=\psi^{\mu}_{A}+\theta_{A}B^{\mu}-\mathrm{i}\rho^{a}_{AB}\theta^{B}\partial _{a}X^{\mu}+\mathrm{i}\bar{\theta}^{B}\theta^{C}\rho^{a}_{AC}\partial _{a}\psi^{\mu}_{B}.$$
Separately we have for each fields
$$\delta_{A}X^{\mu}=\psi_{A}^{\mu},\delta_{A}\psi^{\mu}_{B}=-\mathrm{i}\rho^{a}_{AB}\partial _{a}X^{\mu}+B^{\mu}\epsilon_{AB},\delta_{A}B^{\mu}=-\mathrm{i}\rho^{a}_{AB}\partial _{a}\psi^{\mu B}.$$
Now we can see this is indeed a supersymmetry that exchanges bosonic field with fermionic field, despite the extra $B^{\mu}$.

To write a supersymmetry-invariant action, let's introduce the superspace covariant derivative
$$D_{A}= \frac{\partial }{\partial \bar{\theta}_{A}}-\mathrm{i}\rho^{a}_{AB}\theta^{B}\partial_{a}$$
and $\bar{D}_{A}=(D^{\dagger})^{B} \rho^{0}_{AB}$. It obeys
$$\{ D_{A},Q_{B} \}=0,\{ D_{A},\bar{D}_{B} \}=2\mathrm{i}\rho^{a}_{AB}\partial _{a}.$$
Explicitly we have
$$D_{A}Y_{\mu}=\psi_{\mu A}+\theta_{A}B_{\mu}-\mathrm{i}\rho_{AB}^{a}\theta^{B}\partial _{a}X_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}\bar{\theta}\theta \rho^{a}_{AB}\partial _{a}\psi^{B}_{\mu}.$$
$$\bar{D}^{A}Y^{\mu}=\bar{\psi}^{\mu A}+B^{\mu}\bar{\theta}^{A}+\mathrm{i}\bar{\theta}_{B}\rho^{aAB}\partial _{a}X^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}\bar{\theta}\theta \partial _{a}\psi^{\mu}_{B} \rho^{aAB}.$$
The action generalized from [[Polyakov Action|Polyakov action]] is
$$\begin{align}
S&=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}\int \mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma \mathrm{d}^{2}\theta \bar{D}^{A}Y^{\mu}D_{A}Y_{\mu} \\
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int \mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma (\partial _{a}X_{\mu}\partial ^{a}X^{\mu}-\mathrm{i}\bar{\psi}^{\mu}_{A}\rho^{a}_{AB}\partial _{a}\psi_{\mu B}-B^{\mu}B_{\mu}).
\end{align}$$
One sees that $B_{\mu}$ is not dynamic, thus an auxiliary field. 

The supersymmetry is a global symmetry, thus it gives rise to a [[Noether Theorem|Noether current]] as
$$J^{a}_{A}=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{b}_{AB}(\rho^{a})^{BC}\psi^{\mu}_{C}\partial _{b}X_{\mu}.$$
Additionally, the energy-momentum tensor on the world-sheet is now
$$T_{ab}=\partial _{a}X_{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\mu}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4} \bar{\psi}^{\mu}\rho_{(a}\partial _{b)}\psi_{\mu}-(\text{trace}).$$
The Weyl invariance is preserved, thus $T_{ab}$ is still traceless. The supercurrent also satisfies some constraints as $\rho^{a}_{AB}J_{a}^{B}=0$ 

## Classic theory
By the above action, one can obtain the EOM for fermions
$$\left( \frac{\partial }{\partial \sigma}+\frac{\partial }{\partial \tau} \right)\psi^{\mu}_{-}=0,\left( \frac{\partial }{\partial \sigma}-\frac{\partial }{\partial \tau} \right)\psi^{\mu}_{+}=0,$$
or in lightcone coordinates:
$$\partial _{+}\psi^{\mu}_{-}=\partial _{-}\psi_{+}^{\mu}=0.$$
Similar to bosonic theory, we desire to find the boundary conditions. The constraints here is
$$J_{+}=J_{-}=T_{--}=T_{++}=0.$$
### Boundary conditions and mode expansions
The boundary conditions and mode expansions for $X^{\mu}$ are totally unchanged from before, so here we focus on $\psi^{\mu}$.

For open string, the vanishing of surface term requires that $\psi_{+}\delta \psi_{+}-\psi_{-}\delta \psi_{-}=0$ at each end of open string. This is satisfied by making $\psi_{+}=\pm \psi_{-}$. So without generality, we first set $\psi_{+}^{\mu}(0,\tau)=\psi_{-}^{\mu}(0,\tau)$. Next one has two cases:
1. Ramond (R) boundary conditions $\psi^{\mu}_{+}(\pi,\tau)=\psi^{\mu}_{-}(\pi ,\tau)$, the according mode expansions are $$\psi_{-}^{\mu}(\sigma^{-})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }} \sum_{n\in \mathbb{Z}} d_{n}^{\mu} \mathrm{e}^{ -\mathrm{i}n\sigma^{-} },\psi_{+}^{\mu}(\sigma^{+})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }} \sum_{n\in \mathbb{Z}} d_{n}^{\mu} \mathrm{e}^{ -\mathrm{i}n\sigma^{+} }.$$
2. Neveu-Schwarz (NS) boundary conditions $\psi^{\mu}_{+}(\pi,\tau)=-\psi^{\mu}_{-}(\pi ,\tau)$, the according mode expansions are $$\psi_{-}^{\mu}(\sigma^{-})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }} \sum_{r\in \mathbb{Z}+1 /2} b_{r}^{\mu} \mathrm{e}^{ -\mathrm{i}r\sigma^{-} },\psi_{+}^{\mu}(\sigma^{+})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }} \sum_{n\in \mathbb{Z}+1 /2} b_{r}^{\mu} \mathrm{e}^{ -\mathrm{i}r\sigma^{+} }.$$

For closed strings, the periodic conditions are also these two types. So in total there are R-R, R-NS, NS-R, NS-NS four classes of periodic conditions for spinors on closed strings. The mode expansions for right and left-moving modes are
$$\psi_{-}^{\mu}(\sigma^{-})=\left\{ \begin{align}
&\sum_{n\in \mathbb{Z}} d_{n}^{\mu} \mathrm{e}^{ -2\mathrm{i}n\sigma^{-} } &(\text{R})\\
&\sum_{r\in \mathbb{Z}+1 /2} b_{r}^{\mu} \mathrm{e}^{ -2\mathrm{i}r\sigma^{-} } &(\text{NS})
\end{align}  \right.$$
$$\psi_{+}^{\mu}(\sigma^{+})=\left\{ \begin{align}
&\sum_{n\in \mathbb{Z}} \tilde{d}_{n}^{\mu} \mathrm{e}^{ -2\mathrm{i}n\sigma^{+} } &(\text{R})\\
&\sum_{r\in \mathbb{Z}+1 /2} \tilde{b}_{r}^{\mu} \mathrm{e}^{ -2\mathrm{i}r\sigma^{+} } &(\text{NS})
\end{align}  \right. $$

In bosonic string theory, the Fourier modes of $T_{ab}$ generates the Virasoro algebra. Here, we need Fourier modes of $T_{ab}$ and $J_{a}$ to construct the super-Virasoro algebra:
$$L_{m}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \mathrm{d\sigma}\mathrm{e}^{ \mathrm{i}m\sigma }T_{++}$$
and 
$$F_{m}=\frac{\sqrt{ 2 }}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\sigma \mathrm{e}^{ \mathrm{i}m\sigma }J_{+},G_{r}=\frac{\sqrt{ 2 }}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \mathrm{d}\sigma \mathrm{e}^{ \mathrm{i}r\sigma }J_{+}$$
where the former for R case, latter for NS case. In the case of closed string, there are two sets of super-Virasoro generators. One is given by $T_{++}$ and $J_{+}$ and the other by $T_{--}$ and $J_{-}$.

## Covariant quantization
As before, here we discuss the old covariant quantization, including how we remove negative-norm states to obtain the critical dimension. First, let's promote the Fourier modes of fields into operators with commutation rules:
$$[\alpha_{m}^{\mu},\alpha_{n}^{\nu}]=m\delta_{m+n}\eta^{\mu \nu},\{ b_{r}^{\mu},b_{s}^{\nu} \}=\eta^{\mu \nu}\delta_{r+s},\{ d_{n}^{\mu},d_{n}^{\nu} \}=\eta^{\mu \nu}\delta_{m+n}.$$
For closed strings you have another sets. Again, the zero modes gives the mass-shell condition
$$\alpha'M^{2}=N+a$$
where $a$ is included for normal-ordering effect. Here $N$ is the total particle number operator as $N=N^{(\mathrm{b})}+N^{(\mathrm{f})}$ for
$$N^{\mathrm{(b)}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \alpha_{-m}\cdot\alpha_{m},N^{(\mathrm{f})}=\left\{ \begin{align}
&\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} nd_{-n}\cdot d_{n} &(\text{R}) \\ &\sum_{r=1 /2}^{\infty} rb_{-r}\cdot b_{r} & (\text{NS})
\end{align}    \right.$$
The vacuum of the Fock space is the state that annihilates by every positive modes.

Note that the zero mode in R sector satisfies $\{ d_{0}^{\mu},d_{0}^{\nu} \}=\eta^{\mu \nu}$, which differs from the [[Clifford Algebra|Clifford algebra]] by a normalization. Thus one can conclude that this mode carries a spinor structure in the target space (spacetime). Since $d_{0}^{\mu}$ also commutes with $M^{2}$, so that the states at each mass level furnish a representation of Clifford algebra, thus are spacetime fermions.

### Super-Virasoro algebra and physical states
Now we turn to the quantized super-Virasoro algebra and discuss how they determine physical states. First of all, the generators in the form of Fourier modes are
$$L_{m}=L_{m}^{(\mathrm{b})}+L_{m}^{(\mathrm{f})}$$
where
$$L_{m}^{(\mathrm{b})}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\in \mathbb{Z}} :\alpha_{-m}\cdot\alpha_{m}:,\quad L^{(\mathrm{f})}_{m}=\left\{  \begin{align}
&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\in \mathbb{Z}} \left( n+\frac{1}{2}m \right):d_{-n}\cdot d_{m+n}: &(\mathrm{R} ) \\
&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in \mathbb{Z} +1 /2} \left( r+\frac{1}{2}m \right):b_{-r}\cdot b_{m+r}:&(\mathrm{NS})
\end{align} \right.$$
Next the fermionic generators are
$$\begin{align}
G_{r}&=\sum_{n\in \mathbb{Z}} \alpha_{-n}\cdot b_{r+n}&(\mathrm{NS}), \\
F_{m}&=\sum_{n\in \mathbb{Z}} \alpha_{-n}\cdot d_{m+n}&(\mathrm{R}).
\end{align}$$

The super-Virasoro algebra in NS sector is
$$\begin{align}
[L_{m},L_{n}]&=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{1}{8}D(m^{3}-m)\delta_{m+n} \\
[L_{m},G_{r}]&=\left( \frac{1}{2}m-r \right)G_{m+r} \\
\{ G_{r},G_{s} \}&=2L_{r+s}+\frac{1}{2}D\left( r^{2}-\frac{1}{4} \right)\delta_{r+s}.
\end{align}$$
For R sector one has
$$\begin{align}
[L_{m},L_{n}]&=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{1}{8}Dm^{3}\delta_{m+n} \\
[L_{m},F_{n}]&=\left( \frac{1}{2}m-n \right)F_{m+n} \\
\{ F_{m},F_{n} \}&=2L_{m+n}+\frac{1}{2}Dm^{2}\delta_{m+n}.
\end{align}$$

As before, we ease the constraints to annihilation of physical states by these Fourier modes:
$$G_{r}\ket{\phi}=0,(L_{0}-a)\ket{\phi}=0,L_{n}\ket{\phi}=0,(n>0).    $$
The critical value of $a,D$ that cancel all ghosts is $a=1 /2$ and $D=10$. For R sector, the conditions are
$$(F_{0}-\mu)\ket{\phi}=0,(L_{0}-\mu^{2})\ket{\phi}=0,F_{m}\ket{\phi} =0L_{n}\ket{\phi}=0,(n,m>0).    $$
Here the critical dimension is also $10$.
