Let $\mathbf{C}$ be a [[Category|category]] and $x\in \mathrm{ob}(\mathbf{C})$ be its [[Object|object]]. 

Two [[Monomorphism|monomorphisms]] into $x$ as $f_{1}:s_{1}\hookrightarrow  x$ and $f_{2}:s_{2}\hookrightarrow x$ is [[Equivalence Relation|equivalent]] iff there exists an [[Isomorphism|isomorphism]] $g:s_{1}\leftrightarrow s_{2}$ such that $f_{1}=f_{2}\circ g$. The according [[Equivalence Relation|equivalence class]] is called the **subobject** $\mathrm{sub}(x)$ of $x$.

Two [[Epimorphism|epimorphisms]] from $x$ as $f_{1}:x\twoheadrightarrow  s_{1}$ and $f_{2}:x\twoheadrightarrow s_{2}$ is [[Equivalence Relation|equivalent]] iff there exists an [[Isomorphism|isomorphism]] $g:s_{1}\leftrightarrow s_{2}$ such that $f_{1}=g\circ f_{2}$. The according equivalence class is called the **quotient object** $\mathrm{quo}(x)$of $x$.
